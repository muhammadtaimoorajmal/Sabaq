# Interview Guide: Students

**Length:** 30–40 minutes · **Format:** semi-structured, one participant, one interviewer (plus a note-taker if possible) · **Covers:** RQ1–RQ3, RQ5, RQ6 and hypotheses H1, H2, H3, H5

The questions below are a guide, not a script. Follow interesting answers, use the probes, and skip what has already been answered.

## Before you start

- [ ] Consent obtained ([script](consent-and-privacy.md#consent-script)); recording only with explicit permission.
- [ ] Participant code assigned (P1, P2 …) and written on the [notes template](templates/interview-notes.md), not the name.
- [ ] Phone on silent; a quiet place where the participant feels comfortable.

## Interviewer principles

| Do | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| Ask about **specific past events** ("the last time…") | Asking about hypothetical futures ("would you use…?") |
| Ask open questions ("how", "what happened", "walk me through") | Yes/no and leading questions ("Isn't it annoying when…?") |
| Ask to be **shown** on their phone or laptop | Relying only on what people remember |
| Stay neutral; silence is fine | Explaining Sabaq or its features (it biases every answer) |
| Note exact quotes in quotation marks | Paraphrasing feelings into your own words |

## S1 · Introduction (2 min)

> "Thank you for your time. I'm researching how students keep track of coursework: deadlines, announcements, submissions. There are no right or wrong answers; I'm interested in what really happens, including the messy parts. You can skip any question or stop at any time."

## S2 · Warm-up (3 min)

1. What are you studying, and which year are you in?
2. How many courses are you taking this term? Which one keeps you busiest?
3. What does a typical study day look like for you?

## S3 · How information reaches you (8 min) · RQ1, H1

1. Think of the most recent assignment or lab you were given. **Walk me through how you found out about it**, from the first moment you heard of it.
2. Where did you learn the deadline? Where did you learn *how* to submit it?
3. *(Ask to see)* Could you show me where that information is on your phone right now? How long does it take you to find it?
4. How many chat groups do you have for your courses? How often do you open them?
5. When information in two places disagrees (for example, chat says Friday and the LMS says Thursday), what do you do?

**Probes:** "What happened next?" · "Who told you?" · "How did you know it was correct?"

## S4 · When things went wrong (10 min) · RQ2, H1, H2

Critical-incident questions: ask for **real, specific** stories.

1. Tell me about the **last time you nearly missed, or did miss, a deadline**. What happened?
   - How did you find out? How did you feel?
   - What, if anything, could have warned you earlier?
2. Have you ever **lost marks for something other than the quality of your work**: a wrong file name, wrong format, uploading in the wrong place, uploading a few minutes late?
   - What happened? How much did it cost?
   - How did you find out the rule existed?
3. Before you submit something, **how do you check you have followed the instructions**? *(Ask to see, if possible.)*

**Probes:** "Can you remember roughly when that was?" · "Did that happen to classmates too?"

## S5 · Workload across courses (5 min) · RQ3, H3

1. Think of the **busiest week** you've had this term. When did you realize it was going to be busy?
2. How far ahead do you usually know what's coming?
3. How do you decide what to work on first when several things are due?

## S6 · Tools and workarounds (6 min) · RQ5, H5

1. What do you use to keep track of what's due? *(Paper, notes app, calendar, the LMS, screenshots, pinned messages, memory, a friend…)* **Show me.**
2. Have you ever tried a planner, calendar or to-do app for your studies and stopped using it? What made you stop?
3. Is there a person you rely on to remind you of things? How does that work?

## S7 · Context and wrap-up (4 min) · RQ6

1. Which device do you mostly use for coursework? How is your internet connection on campus and at home?
2. Do you change any settings to make your phone or computer easier to use, such as larger text, dark mode or another language?
3. If you could change **one thing** about how you receive and manage course information, what would it be?
4. Is there anything I didn't ask that I should have?

> "Thank you, this is really helpful. Everything you said stays anonymous."

# Contextual Inquiry Guide

**Length:** 45–90 minutes per session · **Covers:** RQ1, RQ2, RQ3, RQ5, RQ6

Contextual inquiry means watching people do real work **where they normally do it**, and asking about it as it happens. It reveals what interviews miss: the screenshots nobody mentions, the friend everyone messages before submitting, the five apps open at once.

## The four principles

| Principle | In practice |
| --- | --- |
| **Context** | Go to where the work happens. Observe real tasks, not a demonstration. |
| **Partnership** | The participant is the expert; you are the apprentice. Ask them to teach you how they do it. |
| **Interpretation** | When you think you understand something, say it back and let them correct you: "So you screenshot it because…?" |
| **Focus** | Keep the research questions in mind. Here: how course information is found, trusted, acted on and submitted. |

## Where and when to observe

Choose moments when coursework information is actually being handled:

| Setting | Good moment | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Library or study room | A planned study session | Planning, prioritizing, checking what is due |
| Cafeteria or common room | Between classes | Quick checks of chat groups, asking friends |
| Anywhere, close to a deadline | The hour before a submission is due | Submission checks, last-minute confusion, mistakes |
| Hostel room or home (only if invited) | Evening work | Real tool setup, distractions, connectivity |

Only observe people who have consented. Do not record other people in the space.

## How a session runs

1. **Set-up (5 min):** consent; explain you will mostly watch and occasionally ask "why?"; ask them to think aloud if it feels natural.
2. **Observation (30–75 min):** they do their real work; you take notes on the [observation log](templates/observation-log.md).
3. **Interpretation check (5–10 min):** walk through your notes together; confirm or correct each interpretation.
4. **Wrap-up:** thank them; ask whether you may keep any (redacted) artifacts.

## What to look for

- **Channels:** which apps and documents are opened, in what order, and how often.
- **Breakdowns:** hesitation, searching, scrolling back through chat, asking someone, errors, visible frustration.
- **Workarounds:** screenshots, pinned or starred messages, notes to self, alarms, a friend as a reminder service.
- **Trust signals:** what makes them believe one source over another.
- **Physical context:** device, screen size, battery and connection, noise, interruptions.
- **Accessibility:** text size, dark mode, zooming, holding the phone close, language settings.

## Note-taking rules

- Write **observations** and **interpretations** in separate columns. "Scrolled back through chat for 40 seconds" is an observation; "couldn't find the deadline" is an interpretation until the participant confirms it.
- Timestamp each note.
- Put direct quotes in quotation marks.
- Never write names, phone numbers or student IDs.
